An organization processes credit card transactions and must comply with industry security standards. Which of the following is the MOST applicable compliance framework?
A
ISO/IEC 27001 — Information Security Management System
B
NIST SP 800-53 — Security Controls for Federal Systems
C
PCI DSS — Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard
D
SOC 2 Type II — Service Organization Control Report
💡PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) is specifically mandated for any organization that stores, processes, or transmits cardholder data. It is governed by the PCI Security Standards Council (founded by Visa, Mastercard, Amex, etc.). Non-compliance can result in fines and loss of ability to process card payments. ISO 27001 is a general ISMS standard; NIST 800-53 is for US federal systems; SOC 2 is for service organizations.

Which of the following BEST describes the concept of 'privacy by design' as it relates to information security?
A
Encrypting all personal data at rest and in transit after collection
B
Building privacy protections into systems and processes from the beginning, not as an afterthought
C
Implementing a privacy policy and publishing it on the organization website
D
Hiring a Data Protection Officer to oversee all data handling activities
💡Privacy by Design (PbD) — developed by Ann Cavoukian — requires that privacy be embedded into the design of systems, not bolted on afterward. It has 7 foundational principles including: proactive not reactive, privacy as the default, full functionality, end-to-end security, and respect for user privacy. GDPR Article 25 mandates Data Protection by Design and by Default. This is the opposite of adding privacy controls after a product is built.

A
Different countries use incompatible encryption standards
B
Conflicting legal requirements may make simultaneous compliance difficult or impossible
C
International data transfer always requires government approval
D
Security certifications obtained in one country are never recognized in others
💡Jurisdictional conflicts occur when laws of different countries impose incompatible obligations. Example: GDPR restricts data transfers outside the EU, but US law (CLOUD Act) may compel US companies to provide data stored anywhere. Organizations must perform legal analysis, use mechanisms like Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) or Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs), and sometimes make difficult legal tradeoffs when laws directly conflict.

Which of the following CORRECTLY describes the difference between a threat and a vulnerability in risk management?
A
A threat is an internal weakness; a vulnerability is an external danger
B
A threat is a potential danger that could exploit a vulnerability; a vulnerability is a weakness that could be exploited
C
A threat and vulnerability are the same concept expressed differently
D
A vulnerability is always technical; a threat is always human
💡Core risk terminology: Asset = what you protect. Threat = any potential danger or actor that could cause harm (e.g., hacker, fire, employee error). Vulnerability = a weakness in the asset or control that a threat can exploit (e.g., unpatched software, weak password). Risk = the probability that a threat exploits a vulnerability × the resulting impact. All three must be present for risk to exist.

A
Risk rated as High, Medium, or Low based on expert judgment
B
A heat map showing risk likelihood versus impact
C
Annualized Loss Expectancy of $240,000 for a ransomware scenario
D
A risk register with descriptive risk ratings from a Delphi panel
💡Quantitative risk analysis produces specific numeric values — dollars, percentages, frequencies. ALE = $240,000 is a quantitative output that enables direct cost-benefit analysis: if a control costs $50,000/year and prevents $240,000 in expected losses, it justifies the investment. Qualitative outputs are descriptive: High/Medium/Low, heat maps, risk registers with ratings. The Delphi method is a qualitative technique using expert consensus.

08
A security team is implementing controls based on the principle that systems should only be accessible to authorized users and should fail to a secure, restricted state. Which security design principle does this represent?
A
Open design — security should not depend on secrecy of design
B
Fail-safe defaults — default access should be deny unless explicitly permitted
C
Economy of mechanism — keep security designs simple
D
Least common mechanism — minimize shared resources
💡Fail-safe defaults (Saltzer and Schroeder, 1975) means the default state is denial of access — subjects must be explicitly granted access, not explicitly denied it. Example: a firewall that blocks all traffic by default (default-deny) implements fail-safe defaults. If a system fails or a rule is missing, it fails closed (secure) rather than open (insecure). This is different from open design (Kerckhoffs principle) which states security should not rely on keeping the algorithm secret.

09
An organization outsources its payroll processing to a third-party vendor. From a risk management perspective, what is the organization PRIMARY responsibility?
A
Risk is fully transferred to the vendor once the contract is signed
B
The organization retains accountability and must ensure the vendor meets security requirements
C
The vendor assumes all legal liability for any data breaches
D
The organization has no security obligations once outsourcing is complete
💡Outsourcing does NOT transfer accountability. The organization remains ultimately accountable to its customers, regulators, and stakeholders for protecting data — even when processed by a third party. Requirements: conduct vendor due diligence, include security requirements in contracts, perform regular audits/assessments, require evidence of compliance (SOC 2, ISO 27001). Under GDPR, the data controller (organization) remains liable even when a data processor (vendor) causes a breach.

Which of the following activities is MOST associated with the 'preparation' phase of an incident response plan?
A
Identifying the root cause of a security breach
B
Restoring affected systems from clean backups
C
Establishing an incident response team and developing response procedures before incidents occur
D
Notifying affected customers about a data breach
💡The Preparation phase (NIST IR Step 1) happens BEFORE any incident. It includes: establishing and training the Computer Security Incident Response Team (CSIRT), developing IR procedures and playbooks, setting up communication channels, acquiring tools, and defining escalation paths. All other options describe activities in later phases: root cause = Eradication; restoring = Recovery; customer notification = Post-Incident/Lessons Learned.

11
What is the PRIMARY security concern with using a single sign-on (SSO) system?
A
SSO systems are too complex for users to understand
B
SSO increases the attack surface because compromising one credential grants access to all connected systems
C
SSO systems always transmit passwords in cleartext
D
SSO prevents the use of multi-factor authentication
💡SSO risk: if an attacker compromises the SSO credential (or the SSO provider itself), they gain access to ALL connected applications simultaneously — this is called a single point of failure for authentication. Mitigations include: mandatory MFA on SSO, strong session management, and monitoring for anomalous SSO activity. Benefits of SSO (user convenience, reduced password fatigue) must be weighed against this concentration risk. SSO can absolutely work with MFA.

12
A risk assessment determines that the cost to implement a control exceeds the value of the asset being protected. What is the MOST appropriate risk response?
A
Implement the control anyway to demonstrate due care
B
Transfer the risk by purchasing insurance equal to the asset value
C
Accept the risk and document the decision with management approval
D
Avoid the risk by immediately decommissioning the asset
💡Risk acceptance is appropriate when the cost of mitigation exceeds the potential loss. This is a rational economic decision — not negligence — provided it is: formally documented, reviewed and approved by management, and periodically re-evaluated. Simply ignoring a risk without documentation is NOT acceptable. Avoidance (decommissioning) is excessive if the asset still provides business value. Insurance may not cover the full cost. The key: documented, management-approved acceptance.

An organization wants to ensure that a critical employee absence does not disrupt business operations. Which control BEST addresses this concern?
A
Separation of duties — dividing tasks among multiple employees
B
Job rotation — periodically moving employees between roles
C
Cross-training and knowledge documentation to eliminate single points of failure
D
Mandatory vacation — requiring employees to take time off
💡Cross-training (also called succession planning or knowledge transfer) ensures multiple people can perform critical functions. Combined with knowledge documentation (runbooks, SOPs), it eliminates key person dependency — the risk that operations stop when one person is unavailable. Mandatory vacation detects fraud. Job rotation also helps but primarily detects fraud and broadens skills. Separation of duties prevents fraud but doesn't address absence risk.

15
When classifying information, which factor MOST determines the appropriate classification level?
A
The physical size of the document or file
B
The cost to reproduce the information
C
The potential impact to the organization if the information is disclosed, altered, or lost
D
The number of people who currently have access to the information
💡Classification is based on impact — specifically, what happens if the information loses confidentiality (disclosed), integrity (altered), or availability (lost). Higher impact = higher classification = stronger controls required. Example: a government document revealing intelligence sources causes catastrophic national security damage = TOP SECRET. A public press release has no impact if disclosed = UNCLASSIFIED. Current access count and reproduction cost are irrelevant to classification.

Which of the following BEST describes the role of a Security Operations Center (SOC)?
A
Developing and approving organizational security policies
B
Continuously monitoring, detecting, analyzing, and responding to security incidents
C
Conducting annual penetration tests on all organizational systems
D
Managing user access provisioning and deprovisioning
💡A SOC (Security Operations Center) is a centralized team responsible for continuous 24/7 security monitoring using tools like SIEM, IDS/IPS, and threat intelligence feeds. Core SOC functions: monitor alerts, triage events, investigate incidents, escalate and respond, and perform threat hunting. SOCs do NOT set policy (that is governance), conduct penetration tests (that is red team), or manage access (that is IAM). SOC = eyes on glass, detect and respond.

18
An employee is terminated and their access is immediately revoked. Which security concept does this PRIMARILY support?
A
Defense in depth — multiple layers of security
B
Least privilege — limiting access to what is needed
C
Timely account management as part of the identity lifecycle
D
Separation of duties — dividing critical tasks
💡Identity and access lifecycle management requires that access be revoked immediately upon termination to prevent: data theft by disgruntled former employees, unauthorized system access, compliance violations (ex-employees accessing regulated data). While least privilege is related, the PRIMARY concept here is timely lifecycle management — removing access when the business justification ends.

19
A CISSP professional discovers their employer is engaged in activities that violate privacy laws, putting customer data at risk. According to the (ISC)² Code of Ethics, what is the MOST appropriate first action?
A
Immediately report the violation to regulatory authorities
B
Resign from the organization to avoid personal liability
C
Raise the concern internally through appropriate channels, escalating if not resolved
D
Ignore the issue as it is a business decision beyond security scope
💡The (ISC)² Code of Ethics requires protecting society (Canon 1) and acting honorably (Canon 2). The appropriate process: (1) Raise concern internally first — give the organization opportunity to correct itself. (2) If internal channels fail or are blocked, escalate to senior management or legal/compliance. (3) If still unresolved and harm is imminent, reporting to authorities may be required. Immediate external reporting without internal escalation is premature. Resignation protects you but not the public. Ignoring violates Canon 1.
