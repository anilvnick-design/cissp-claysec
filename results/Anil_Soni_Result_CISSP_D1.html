A healthcare organisation's Board states: 'We will accept significant financial risk to expand market share, but we will accept ZERO risk of patient data exposure.' This statement defines what?
A
Risk tolerance — operational variance limits around specific processes
B
Risk threshold — the point at which risk triggers automatic escalation
C
Risk capacity — the maximum risk the organisation can absorb financially
D
Risk appetite — the board-level strategic declaration of acceptable risk
💡Risk Appetite = the broad, strategic, TOTAL amount of risk an organisation is WILLING to accept in pursuit of objectives — set by the Board. It is qualitative and directional. Risk Capacity = maximum risk the org CAN absorb (financial/operational ceiling). Risk Threshold = the trigger point for escalation. The Board's strategic declaration = Risk Appetite.

Each critical system should have its own ISCP. DRP = covers IT INFRASTRUCTURE PORTFOLIO — all systems, data centres, networks, telecommunications. The DRP calls the individual ISCPs. Hierarchy: BCP (enterprise) → DRP (IT infrastructure) → ISCP (specific system). Hospital analogy: DRP = 'how do we recover our entire data centre.' ISCP = 'how do we recover the EHR system specifically with its 4-hour RTO.' The ISCP is the child document; DRP is the parent.

RPO = 15 minutes; data lost = 2 minutes (10:45 to 10:47); RPO was exceeded

RPO = 15 minutes; data lost = 2 minutes (10:45 to 10:47); RPO was met — 2 minutes is within the 15-minute objective
C
RPO = 2 minutes; data lost = 15 minutes; RPO was exceeded
D

💡RPO (Recovery Point Objective) = the maximum amount of data loss measured in TIME that is acceptable — determined during BIA based on business impact of losing X minutes/hours of data. Trading platform backup every 15 minutes = RPO = 15 minutes. Failure at 10:47, last backup 10:45 = 2 minutes of data lost. 2 minutes < 15 minute RPO = RPO was met. If failure had occurred at 10:59 (just before 11:00 backup), data loss = 14 minutes — still within RPO. RPO drives backup frequency: tighter RPO = more frequent backups. After restoring a compromised web application (RTO met at 4 hours), the team spends 2 hours: running integrity verification, testing all user functions, clearing security alerts, and confirming no reinfection before going live. The MTD is 7 hours. Was MTD met, and what does the 2-hour post-restoration phase represent?
A
MTD was met (4+2=6 < 7); the post-restoration phase is part of RTO
B
MTD was missed — any post-restoration testing resets the RTO clock
C
MTD was met (4+2=6 < 7); the 2-hour post-restoration phase = WRT (Work Recovery Time)
D
MTD was met; the testing phase is part of incident response, not BCP metrics
💡WRT (Work Recovery Time) = the time required AFTER the system is technically restored to validate, test, and verify it is safe and fully functional before returning to production. This is separate from RTO. MTD = RTO + WRT. Calculation: RTO = 4 hours (system restored). WRT = 2 hours (testing and verification). Total = 6 hours. MTD = 7 hours. 6 hours < 7 hours = MTD met ✓. Why WRT matters: a system restored but not verified may be reinfected, misconfigured, or incomplete. Returning to production prematurely could cause secondary incidents. WRT must be planned as part of DRP/ISCP — underestimating WRT causes MTD breaches even when RTO is met.
